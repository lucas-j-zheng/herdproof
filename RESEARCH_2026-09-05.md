# HerdProof — Further Research and Claim Revision

**Research date: 2026-09-05.** Primary sources were checked for counting,
capture integrity, livestock handling, lender workflows, competitors, and
implementation licensing. This is a research assessment plus a small local
metadata experiment, not a cattle-model benchmark or customer interview study.

Current specification: [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).
Implementation verdict and experiment status: [FEASIBILITY.md](FEASIBILITY.md).

## 1. Conclusion

An assisted batch-review tool is technically plausible and worth testing.
A general service that proves an arbitrary pledged herd from borrower-supplied
video is not established by the available evidence.

The feasible chain is:

**defined handling batch -> filmed passage proposals -> full-clip human review ->
same-batch record comparison -> evidence export and illustrative calculation.**

Each step has a narrower meaning than whole-herd collateral verification.
Production value depends on whether the resulting evidence saves work for a
lender or inspector. Neither cheaper lending nor replaced inspections has been
demonstrated.

## 2. Is counting technically possible?

### Evidence supporting a prototype

The Uruguay dataset publisher describes phone videos, a manually corrected MOT
sequence, and a CC BY 4.0 release. Initial model boxes and track IDs needed human
correction. That supports accessible evaluation material and the review workflow,
not an assumption that stock tracking is already accurate.
[Dataset publisher](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/dk54zg67dd/1)

The existing project audit found file/annotation discrepancies and reported
9 identities seen, 7 midpoint crossers, and 6 completed traversals. These results
are from annotation tracks. The observation definition must be frozen before
calling any of these numbers ground truth for a counting task. Source-video
coverage and boundary censoring matter. [Prior audit](DATASET_SEARCH.md)

### Evidence against a plug-and-play guarantee

CattleEyeView reports 14 fixed overhead loading-ramp sequences. Its Table III
gives passage-count MAE of 20.5 for BoT-SORT and 24.0 for ByteTrack and attributes
errors partly to repeated line crossing. These are results of that paper's
specific setup, not expected HerdProof error rates. They demonstrate why detector
quality and final counting accuracy need separate evaluation.
[Paper, experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08764v1#S4)

Newer work offers evidence of technical progress: a 2025 four-camera black-cattle
study reports 95.61% MOTA over ten cases. It uses customized segmentation and
association with a prepared camera setup. MOTA is a tracking metric; it is not
95.61% exact herd-count accuracy or proof that one phone view works equally well.
[Scientific Reports study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91553-4)

**Design inference:** fixed geometry, directional event rules, and human review
are a credible first implementation. Neither a state machine nor a newer detector
automatically solves ID switches, animals missed entirely, or recirculation
outside the camera view.

### Why the earlier uncertainty bound was withdrawn

This is a logical limitation of the proposed estimator, independent of any one
paper:

- Two high-confidence tracks can describe the same animal after an ID switch.
  Counting both can overstate unique animals even in the alleged lower bound.
- An animal entirely missed by the detector never appears among low-confidence
  candidates. The proposed upper bound can therefore understate the true number.
- An animal can complete a filmed traversal twice after recirculating out of view.
  Perfect event detection would still not imply two distinct animals.
- A calibrated interval on representative future data would concern a defined
  statistical population and observation protocol, not guarantee completeness
  against an adversarial operator.

Display proposals, unresolved intervals, and reviewed events instead. Unknown
coverage must remain unknown. To avoid missing errors that the model did not flag,
the prototype reviewer watches the entire clip.

## 3. Does the capture workflow make sense on a farm?

Penn State Extension describes receiving procedures that can include treatment
and individual weighing, and explains the holding areas, alleys, chutes, and
loading infrastructure used to handle cattle. Handling still involves people
and animal-flow constraints.
[Extension guidance](https://extension.psu.edu/beef-cattle-facilities-handling-systems)

**Product inference:** capture during an already planned receiving, loading, or
handling session is more plausible than asking a rancher to gather an extensive
herd for an unsolicited short video. Camera time is only one part of total work.

A potential pilot should establish:

- which routine event produces a complete, useful batch;
- how often it occurs relative to the lender's monitoring need;
- whether counted and uncounted animals remain physically separated;
- whether the camera can be mounted without disrupting operations;
- how setup, connectivity, uploads, and recapture affect total time.

No operator interviews were conducted. The suggested workflow is a testable
hypothesis. It does not establish universal farm suitability or low setup cost.

## 4. Would a lender have a use for this?

The OCC's Agricultural Lending handbook discusses monitoring livestock movements
through inventory records, comparing them with inspections, confirming ownership
and possession, and considering factors beyond head count when valuing animals.
It also addresses inspection independence. This supports investigating a
supplemental evidence workflow, not a claim that borrower video satisfies an
inspection requirement.
[OCC handbook, printed pp. 20–22](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/agricultural-lending/pub-ch-agricultural-lending.pdf)

FCA's Collateral Risk Management guidance separately addresses inspection timing,
ownership, multiple pledges, sources of value, and borrowing-base administration.
Its official update archive confirms the September 2025 revision. Direct PDF
retrieval timed out in this review; the relevant official indexed text and update
entry were available.
[FCA guidance](https://ww3.fca.gov/readingrm/exammanual/New%20Exam%20Manual/22.6.pdf),
[revision record](https://www.fca.gov/newsroom/archive-of-updates)

**Product inference:** sell assistance preparing and reviewing a batch evidence
packet. A qualified reviewer still needs to determine what the observation says
about eligible collateral. Being able to see cattle does not resolve a lien or
prove that the borrower owns them.

Questions for a lender or inspection firm, not answered by this research:

1. Which decision would a reviewed batch video change?
2. Would it supplement a report, trigger an inspection, or support an inventory
   adjustment? Who is permitted to rely on it?
3. What evidence of batch identity, independence, and completeness is necessary?
4. How much staff time does the current process take, and what is that time worth?
5. Would video add value when an EID/scale session already exists?

No regulatory endorsement, lender acceptance, fraud reduction, market size, or
willingness-to-pay claim is supported.

## 5. What can capture technology prove?

### Ordinary uploads

An MP4 can carry editable time and location tags. The new local synthetic
experiment changed both without changing the decoded frame sequence. The file
hash changed as a consequence.
[Probe script](scripts/probe_video_metadata.py),
[run results](research/2026-09-05/provenance_probe.json)

Inference: SHA-256 matches exact bytes. It is useful for linking a report to a
file and identifying exact reuse; it is not a detector for all reuse of content.
The experiment did not include a QR or bypass a challenge validator.

A one-use challenge can establish server state and show that a token appears in
the uploaded file. A fresh token can also appear beside a replay screen or be
added to old footage. Server receipt time is distinct from capture time.

### Native app/device attestation

Apple describes App Attest as evidence about the app/device and the integrity of
request payloads. Its fraud-risk documentation also describes limitations with
compromised devices.
[Apple technical explanation](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10244/),
[Apple risk documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/assessing-fraud-risk)

**Design inference:** binding a session and original-media digest to an app
assertion can strengthen the upload path. It does not establish that a camera
filmed the correct animals, that the scene was not staged, or that all animals
were presented. Hardware-backed app integrity is not sensor-to-scene truth.

### Content Credentials and signatures

C2PA explicitly distinguishes verifiable provenance from whether depicted
content is true or accurate.
[C2PA explainer, section 7.2.2](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html)

**Design inference:** a signed report protects the association between a signing
key and the recorded assessment. It needs a separately trusted public key.
Embedding a signature and its replacement public key together does not prevent a
substituted report from appearing self-consistent. Neither a demo signature nor
C2PA proves cattle ownership, complete coverage, or correctness of the count.

### Claim boundaries by scenario

| Scenario | What the MVP can say |
|---|---|
| Exact media reused for another session | Matches a retained media hash; additional evidence needed |
| Same valid submission retried | Return the existing result; do not accuse the user of fraud |
| Token already bound to accepted media | Challenge reuse rejected according to server state |
| Media re-encoded or metadata changed | Byte-match may miss it; freshness remains unproven |
| File reports an in-parcel location | Supplied location is consistent with the configured polygon |
| Current QR added to old footage | No general detection guarantee |
| Another owner's cattle filmed | Ownership remains unverified |
| Same animal circulated out of view | Unique-batch coverage remains unverified |
| Signed assessment verifies | Assessment integrity relative to a trusted key, not truth of inputs |

## 6. Is the idea differentiated?

CattleQuants explicitly markets drone-based cattle counting for lender collateral
verification and offers inspection reports. Its advertised accuracy figures are
vendor claims; this review did not independently validate them.
[Company's description](https://cattlequants.com/why-use-drones-for-livestock-collateral-verification/)

Electronic identification is another baseline. Tru-Test documentation describes
session counting and duplicate-tag handling. The legacy XRS manual's duplicate
check has a stated last-500-tag scope; do not generalize that implementation detail
to all readers.
[Manufacturer's XRS manual](https://us.tru-test.com/sites/default/files/webdam_assets/142083036.pdf)

**Product inference:** the proposed distinction is a convenient review workflow
combining video events, manifest discrepancies, corrections, and export. It is
not an established moat. Compare against EID/scale exports and manual video
review, as well as inspection services. An optional timestamped EID import could
help associate observed passages with records, but tag reads still need reliable
association and do not independently settle ownership.

An indexed Plainsight livestock-counting page was found, but its direct URL
returned 404. Treat it as historical marketing evidence, not a confirmed current
offering or a source of comparative performance claims.
[Indexed page checked](https://plainsight.ai/filters/livestock-counting/)

## 7. Practical implementation constraints

Ultralytics offers AGPL and enterprise licensing paths. An open hackathon
distribution and a proprietary deployment need different planning; being a
hackathon is not itself an exemption.
[Vendor licensing page](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)

YOLOX's repository code is Apache-2.0. This is a candidate alternative, not a
performance finding or blanket permission covering any weights or datasets.
[Repository license](https://github.com/Megvii-BaseDetection/YOLOX/blob/main/LICENSE)

The project does not yet contain an installed, pinned CV application stack.
Prepare pinned dependencies before testing and disable tracker-initiated runtime
installs. Use the user's release-age and execution protections.

Existing licensed footage can support algorithm evaluation. Its historical
capture cannot be bound retrospectively to a newly issued challenge. Test the
submission mechanism using separately labeled synthetic fixtures.

## 9. Smallest decisive feasibility exercise

Before polishing the app:

1. Freeze independent passage labels on a development clip and at least one
   separate evaluation clip if available.
2. Run the detector/tracker/event rules and save actual per-event results.
3. Watch all footage to correct proposals; record review time and missed errors.
4. Compare with manual counting and report preparation.
5. Have an operator assess the capture procedure and a lender/inspector identify
   a specific use for the output.

Suggested gates, not validated performance promises:

- Automatic demo: zero missed and false passage events on its disclosed clip.
- Assisted demo: final reviewed ledger matches independent labels; corrections
  remain visible.
- Product experiment: a material reduction in total review/preparation effort
  without extra animal handling that cancels the benefit.
- Expansion: additional farms, dates, camera views, and adverse cases must be
  evaluated before broad accuracy or time-saving claims.

**Current evidence supports building that experiment. It does not yet establish
an automatic collateral-verification product.**
